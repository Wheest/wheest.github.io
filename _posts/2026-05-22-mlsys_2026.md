In May I headed out to [MLSys 2026](https://mlsys.org/) in Bellevue, Washington,
with my Fractile colleagues Carlo and Stelios.

<!--more-->

There were more papers and talks than I care to list here, but it was good to
validate the direction things had been going in. The pace of innovation is
clearly outstripping the lead times of conference reviews: a lot of what was
presented has already been built on, or landed in production frameworks, since
it was submitted.

It was interesting to see how prevalent agent hill-climbing for kernel
optimisation was. By my rough count, about a third of the papers touched on
agentic optimisation of kernels or mappings. My time in the hand-written kernel
mines in years gone by is clearly an artifact of a bygone era.

A few that stood out, from my rough notes taken during the talks:

### Keynotes

- **Beyond Model Serving: Cross-Stack Co-design for Agentic Systems** (Esha
  Choukse, Microsoft Azure Research). We still treat agentic workloads as tools
  wrapped around model calls, and so optimise the wrong thing. She argued
  against three illusions: that the model is the system, that more compute
  solves accuracy, and that correctness is binary. Case studies were
  [Sherlock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00330) and
  [StreamWise](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05800).
- **Rethinking Pretraining: Data and Architecture** (Luke Zettlemoyer). A meaty
  one on data efficiency, controllable test-time compute, getting rid of the
  tokeniser, and modular or opt-out training.
- **LMCache** (Yuhan Liu). "KV-cache is the new AI-native data." LMCache runs as
  a separate service that vLLM and SGLang connect to, decoupling KV-cache
  management from the inference engine. See also
  [CacheGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240) and
  [CacheBlend](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16444).

### Inference and serving

- [**BLASST**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12087): training-free block-sparse
  attention that reuses FlashAttention's online softmax statistics to skip
  negligible blocks. Roughly 1.6x prefill and 1.5x decode speedups at ~74%
  sparsity, and it has already landed in TensorRT-LLM.
- [**SparseSpec**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01278): self-speculative decoding
  where the draft is the same model with sparse attention, aimed at long
  chain-of-thought reasoning where decode is memory-bound. Up to 2.13x
  throughput, lossless.
- [**Demystifying the Mixture of Experts Serving Tax**](https://openreview.net/forum?id=lELxqcgrsN):
  MoE models run 2–3x worse latency than FLOP-equivalent dense models. Prefill
  suffers from load imbalance; decode from expert activations.
- [**Event Tensor**](https://openreview.net/forum?id=PJqFhAbUHa): a compiler
  abstraction for dynamic megakernels, encoding tile-level dependencies so that
  shape- and data-dependent dynamism can live inside one persistent kernel.
- [**Breaking the Ice**](https://research.ibm.com/publications/breaking-the-ice-analyzing-cold-start-latency-in-vllm):
  a breakdown of vLLM cold-start latency into six stages, from framework
  bootstrap to CUDA graph capture, with a predictor for each.
- [**Optimizing Deployment Configurations for LLM Inference**](https://openreview.net/forum?id=gEbKQeIdxB):
  Meta's retrospective on serving Llama 3. By their own admission nothing
  surprising, but large-scale validation of what the community already
  suspected.

### Tooling and hardware design

- [**XProf**](https://openreview.net/forum?id=KqRLAdGK6C): Google's ML
  accelerator profiler, with the full story of how it integrates with
  frameworks, the compiler and the PJRT runtime.
- [**ExecuTorch**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08195): Meta's on-device runtime,
  lowering from `torch.export` through composable backend delegates. The speaker
  opened with a live demo on his Ray-Ban Meta glasses.
- [**VeriMoA**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27617): a training-free
  mixture-of-agents for generating HDL from a spec, using C++ and Python as
  intermediate languages and a quality-ranked cache across layers.
- [**RocketPPA**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21971): one fine-tuned Llama 3.1
  model predicting power, performance and area straight from RTL, skipping
  synthesis for early design-space exploration.
